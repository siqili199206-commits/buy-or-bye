# Purchase-gate decision framework

Use this framework to determine whether a purchase should happen at all. It is not a product-quality checklist and should not become a mechanical questionnaire.

## 1. Real task

Identify the concrete situation the purchase is meant to improve. A task is stronger when it names a recurring activity, person, environment, or deadline. “I want to become the kind of person who uses this” is a fantasy-self signal, not proof of use.

Useful counterfactual: if the product disappeared from the market tomorrow, what problem would remain?

## 2. Gap strength

Determine whether the user currently cannot perform the task, performs it poorly, or merely expects a marginal upgrade. Compare against owned items, repair, configuration changes, borrowing, renting, and changing the task.

A real gap can still lead to `WAIT`, `BORROW_OR_RENT`, `BUY_USED`, or `REUSE_OR_REPAIR`.

## 3. Plausible use

Estimate use from scheduled or repeated behavior, not aspiration alone:

- When is the first concrete use?
- How often did the underlying activity occur in the last 30–90 days?
- What friction must be overcome each time?
- Where will the item live, charge, be cleaned, or be carried?

Do not demand frequent use from collectibles, gifts, art, or rare-event safety items; judge them against their actual purpose.

## 4. Economic fit

Consider affordability and opportunity cost without moralizing. Include:

- purchase price and financing cost;
- accessories, subscriptions, consumables, maintenance, storage, and resale loss;
- probability-adjusted cost if the item is returned late, underused, or replaced soon;
- cost per meaningful use when that metric is sensible.

Discount percentage is not savings unless the item already passes the purchase gate. Compare against the normal street price rather than an arbitrary list price when reliable history exists.

## 5. Behavioral-pressure check

Look for decision distortion:

- countdowns, low-stock messages, coupon expiry, bundles, or free-shipping thresholds;
- social comparison, influencer repetition, status anxiety, or identity performance;
- mood repair, reward seeking, boredom, or stress;
- upgrade loops driven by novelty rather than task failure;
- sunk-cost reasoning around an ecosystem or collection;
- research fatigue creating pressure to “just decide.”

These signals support a cooling period; they do not automatically prove the purchase is wrong.

Useful tests:

- **Full-price test:** Would the user still want it without the promotion?
- **Invisible-use test:** Would it still be valuable if nobody else saw it?
- **Replacement test:** Which owned item will stop being used?
- **Calendar test:** Name the first three realistic uses.
- **Exit test:** What will the user do if it disappoints?

## 6. Fit and friction

Account for physical fit, compatibility, learning time, setup, portability, maintenance, privacy, and household constraints. A technically superior product can be worse if its friction suppresses actual use.

## 7. Reversibility and evidence

Increase caution when the purchase is expensive, personalized, difficult to return, safety-relevant, subscription-linked, or based on unverified claims. Prefer a demo, fitting, rental, trial, returnable channel, or small real-world experiment when it can resolve the key unknown.

## Selecting the verdict

- Choose `BUY` only when a meaningful gap and plausible use are both supported and no dominant downside remains.
- Choose `WAIT` when time is likely to improve price, information, emotional clarity, or product generation without creating a material task failure.
- Choose `VALIDATE_FIRST` when a cheap test can resolve the dominant unknown.
- Choose `REUSE_OR_REPAIR` when an owned item can meet the real task after a bounded fix or configuration change.
- Choose `BORROW_OR_RENT` for temporary, rare, or exploratory use when ownership adds little value.
- Choose `BUY_USED` when ownership is justified but new-product depreciation, sustainability, or budget makes used materially better and safety/hygiene risk is manageable.
- Choose `DO_NOT_BUY` when the task is vague, the gap is absent, use is implausible, or downside clearly dominates.

## Confidence

Express confidence as `high`, `medium`, or `low`, followed by the reason. Confidence reflects evidence completeness and decision stability, not rhetorical certainty.

If one unknown could flip the verdict, confidence cannot be high. Turn that unknown into the next action.
